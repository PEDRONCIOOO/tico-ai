About the opportunity (must-ask)
1. "Is this role with Symbium directly, or am I contracting through Code in Canada?"
You need to know the structure before anything else. Clarifies who pays you, who manages you, and what entity shows up on the contract.

2. "Symbium's careers page mentions US work authorization and California relocation. What's the arrangement for Brazil-based engineers specifically?"
Direct, respectful, and necessary. If she's sourcing for Symbium despite that restriction, she has an answer — EOR, contractor, remote exception. You need to hear it.

3. "What's the contract model — full-time, fixed-term, open-ended contractor?"
Matters for your planning and for how you price yourself.

4. "What's the expected start date and the overall timeline from here?"
Tells you how serious they are and lets you plan parallel conversations.
About the role (shows seniority)

5. "What portion of the stack would I be touching? The Complaw engine in Prolog seems to be maintained by a specialized team — is this role more on the product surface and integrations?"
Shows you read their material carefully. Also helps you gauge how much of the work is Prolog vs. modern full-stack.

6. "How is the engineering team structured? Would I be reporting to Tristan Krueger as Engineering Lead, or directly to the CTO?"
You did research. You know names. Recruiters notice this instantly.

7. "What's the current engineering team size, and what's the hiring plan for the next six months?"
Tells you whether you'd be the tenth engineer or the twentieth, and whether there's budget pressure.

8. "What does a typical week look like for a senior engineer on the team? What's the ratio of building new features vs. maintaining or scaling existing systems?"
Real question every senior should ask before joining a pre-Series A startup.
About the product (shows domain interest)

9. "How does Symbium handle integrations with local government systems? I imagine each city or county has its own workflow system, so I'm curious about the integration patterns."
You're signaling domain fluency and tech-lead thinking without needing to tell the SERPRO story (which you don't want to center anymore). It's a genuinely interesting engineering question about their product.

10. "How do you handle the fact that regulations change constantly across 50+ jurisdictions? Is there a process for keeping Complaw rules up to date?"
Shows you're thinking about the operational reality of their product, not just the code.

11. "What's the most exciting technical challenge on the roadmap for the next six to twelve months?"
Open-ended, gets her to share real information, and gives you material for follow-up conversations.
About culture and ways of working

12. "What's the working hours overlap expectation with the US team? I'm on BRT, so I want to understand the rhythm."
Practical and important. Symbium is SF-based, so they're likely PT (GMT-7/8). BRT is GMT-3. That's a 4-5 hour gap. Worth knowing if they expect you online till midnight or just morning overlap.

13. "How does Symbium do code review, technical design, and architectural decisions? Is there a formal RFC process or is it more lightweight?"
Senior-level question. Shows you care about how decisions get made.

14. "What's your read on the engineering culture there? What kind of person thrives, and what kind struggles?"
Lets her give you honest signal. Recruiters who've placed people at a company know this answer well.
To close the call

15. "What are the next steps if this conversation goes well?"
Always ask this near the end. Signals you're taking the process seriously.

16. "Is there anything specific you'd recommend I prepare for the technical stage?"
Lets her give you a hint about what's coming. Recruiters often tell you more than they mean to here.

17. "Anything else you'd want to know about me that we haven't covered?"
Gives her space to surface any gap before you end the call, so you don't get surprised by a concern you could have addressed.
